## Key Research Findings by Injury/Topic

### 1. HAMSTRING STRAIN REHABILITATION

**Core Evidence:**
- Bourne et al. 2018 (PMID 29116573, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0796-x)): Evidence-based framework for strengthening exercises. Hip extension exercises (45° machine) preferentially activate biceps femoris long head (BFlh). Nordic curl preferentially activates semitendinosus. Both needed in full hamstring rehab.
- Chu & Rho 2016 (PMID 27172083, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1249/JSR.0000000000000264)): Conservative management involves gradual intensity increase, progressing to sport-specific and neuromuscular control exercises. Eccentric strengthening for proximal hamstring tendinopathy.
- Paton et al. 2023 (Consensus): International Delphi consensus. 89.8% agreement: individualized rehab. 85.5% agreement: PAIN-FREE criteria specifically required for sprinting.

**Nordic Hamstring Exercise (NHE) Evidence:**
- Van der Horst et al. 2015 (PMID 25794868, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546515574057)): RCT - NHE reduced hamstring injury incidence 64% in amateur soccer (OR 0.282, p=0.005)
- Alonso-Fernandez et al. 2017 (PMID 28314091, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12877)): NHE increases BFlh fascicle length (key injury prevention mechanism)
- Goode et al. 2015 (PMID 25227125, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2014-093466)): Meta-analysis. With good compliance: 65% injury risk reduction (RR=0.35)
- Behan et al. 2023 (PMID 36651719, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14317)): RCT on NHE dosage. Low-to-high volume protocol causes most DOMS. Low volume better tolerated.
- Bourne et al. 2016 (PMID 27467123, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-095739)): fMRI study. 45° hip extension exercise selectively targets BF long head vs. Nordic (targets semitendinosus).

**Exercise Selection Rationale:**
- Phase 1-2: Passive stretch, prone curl (pain-free range)
- Phase 2-3: Romanian Deadlift (hip-dominant, BFlh activation), 45° hip extension
- Phase 3-4: Nordic Hamstring Curl (eccentric), progressive running to sprinting
- Evidence level A: Nordic, RDL, progressive running with pain-free sprint criterion

### 2. ACL REHABILITATION

**Core Evidence:**
- van Melick et al. 2016 (Consensus - 772 citations): Evidence-based clinical practice guideline. 3-phase post-op approach: (1) impairment-based, (2) sport-specific training, (3) return to play. Battery of strength + hop tests + psychological tests + movement quality required. 9-12 months rehabilitation.
- Graziano et al. 2017 (Consensus): Criteria-based rehabilitation with qualitative + quantitative assessment. Mean 12-month return. 83% returned to unrestricted activity.
- Kopf et al. 2025 (PMID 41096031, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14196952)): Digital monitoring of 335 ACLR patients. LSI increased from 0.64 → 0.81 in early phases. Early 3 months are critical. Pain negatively impacts performance.
- Fontanet et al. 2025 (PMID 41283557, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk10040450)): BFR training systematic review (15 studies, 417 patients). BFR effective both pre-op and post-op ACLR.

**OKC vs CKC:**
- Closed kinetic chain (squat, leg press) preferred in early phase for reduced ACL graft strain
- Open kinetic chain (leg extension) can be introduced phase 2 (90°-40° range) without increased graft elongation per evidence

**Return-to-Sport Tests:**
- Single-leg hop for distance, triple hop, 6m timed hop, crossover hop (all target ≥90% LSI)
- ACL-RSI psychological readiness ≥65
- Minimum 9 months post-ACLR (reduces re-injury from 40% at 6m to 10% at 9m+)

### 3. ANKLE SPRAIN REHABILITATION

**Core Evidence:**
- De Fazio et al. 2025 (PMID 40818659, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.jfas.2025.08.004)): Prospective study - 119 patients (LAS + CAI). Post-rehabilitation both groups improved ROM, strength, functional scores. However, persistent EMG deficits in CAI indicate incomplete neuromuscular recovery. Personalized neuromuscular interventions required.
- Sadaak et al. 2024 (PMID 38992731, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-024-04855-0)): RCT - Aquatic therapy vs conventional for grade III ankle sprain. Aquatic therapy superior for pain, dynamic balance, agility, and return-to-sport time (significant, p<0.001).
- Foster et al. 2023 (PMID 36084225, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003035)): Military cohort study (33,361). Exercise therapy after ankle sprain reduced subsequent knee injury (HR 0.87), hip injury (HR 0.68), lumbar injury (HR 0.82). Strong case for comprehensive rehabilitation.

**Key Exercises Evidence:**
- Proprioception/balance training: strong evidence for reducing recurrence in chronic ankle instability
- Y-Balance Test: validated measure of dynamic balance and return-to-sport criterion
- Hop tests: widely used for functional clearance

### 4. ROTATOR CUFF REHABILITATION

**Core Evidence:**
- Schwank et al. 2022 (Consensus - 124 citations): Bern Consensus Statement on Shoulder Injury. Load and risk management framework. Criteria-based return-to-sport process.
- Key exercises: ER with Theraband (infraspinatus/teres minor), prone T's/Y's/W's (scapular stabilizers), progressive overhead loading
- Scapular stabilization is critical: 24149210, 29329890

**Evidence Notes:**
- Rotator cuff rehabilitation evidence is primarily expert consensus and observational level (Level B-C)
- Scapular dyskinesis correction is central to treatment ('swimmer's shoulder' protocol: 26359841)
- External rotation:internal rotation strength ratio target: ≥66% ER/IR ratio

### 5. PATELLAR TENDINOPATHY

**Core Evidence:**
- Liu et al. 2026 (PMID 42192475, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01743-4)): Network meta-analysis. 17 RCTs. HSR (heavy slow resistance) is reference treatment. Eccentric overload showed best point estimate (SMD 0.33 vs HSR) but not statistically superior. No intervention clearly superior to HSR.
- Hjortshoej et al. 2025 (PMID 41452311, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.70186)): RCT - BFR vs HSR for 12-week protocol + 52-week follow-up. BOTH equally effective for pain and function. BFR useful when high load is not tolerable.
- Agergaard et al. 2024 (PMID 39430115, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/23259671241280192)): 3-4 year follow-up post loading rehab. VISA-P at 83.9 ± 11.9 at 3.6 years. Clinical symptoms persist; sports participation lower than pre-injury.
- Rieder et al. 2022 (PMID 36338477, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2022.1017931)): WBV equivalent to HSR for VAS improvement, inconclusive for VISA-P.

**Protocol:**
- Phase 1: Isometric at 60° (4-5x45 seconds) - immediate analgesic
- Phase 2-3: HSR (decline squat 3-4x6-15, 3-4 sessions/week, target 70-90% 1RM)
- Phase 4: Plyometric progression. VISA-P outcome measure.

### 6. ACHILLES TENDINOPATHY

**Core Evidence:**
- Silbernagel et al. 2020 (Consensus - 183 citations): Comprehensive review. Progressive tendon loading is central. Fear of movement is important psychological factor. Return-to-sport planning required.
- Radovanovic et al. 2022 (PMID 36538166, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-022-00545-5)): High-load (~90% MVC) exercise superior to Alfredson for tendon mechanical/morphological adaptations (stiffness +20.1%, CSA +8.98%, MVC +7.2%).
- Head et al. 2020 (PMID 31763774, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/msc.1428)): Systematic review. Low-quality evidence that do-as-tolerated modification of Alfredson is more effective than standardized protocol for short-term function.
- Murphy et al. 2018 (PMID 29653591, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-018-0725-6)): PROSPERO-registered protocol: is Alfredson superior to other interventions?
- Sivrika et al. 2023 (PMID 37628466, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare11162268)): Systematic review. Supports eccentric loading based on Alfredson. Pilates as alternative. Can modify pace/intensity.

**Alfredson Protocol:**
- Straight-knee heel drop: 3x15 twice daily = 180 reps/day
- Bent-knee heel drop: 3x15 twice daily
- 12-week protocol; load added when pain-free (backpack with weight)
- Pain allowed during exercise (not a contraindication in Alfredson protocol for mid-portion AT)
- VISA-A outcome measure; target ≥80 for return to sport

### 7. PLANTAR FASCIITIS

**Core Evidence:**
- Ibraheem et al. 2026 (PMID 41715005, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-026-09562-x)): RCT - multimodal rehab (short foot exercise, unilateral heel raising, taping + stretching) vs conventional. Both improved all outcomes. Multimodal superior for foot posture.
- Cil et al. 2020 (PMID 31387381, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1071100719865302)): RCT - Outpatient (clinic with manual therapy + exercise) vs home program. Supervised superior for all outcomes at 8 weeks and 6 months.
- Dunning et al. 2018 (PMID 30379937, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0205405)): RCT - Electrical dry needling + manual therapy + exercise vs manual therapy + exercise alone. Dry needling group significantly superior for pain and function at 3 months.
- Ryskalin et al. 2023 (PMID 37539773, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.24102)): Anatomical review. Soleus/gastrocnemius tightness found in ~80% of PF patients. AT loading directly affects plantar aponeurosis tension.

**Protocol:**
- Plantar fascia-specific stretch (10x10-second holds, every morning and before standing)
- Gastrocnemius + soleus stretching
- Short foot exercise (intrinsic muscle strengthening)
- Progressive calf raises / windlass strengthening
- Manual therapy added for superior outcomes

### 8. GROIN / ADDUCTOR STRAIN

**Core Evidence:**
- Holmich et al. 2011 (PMID 21813441, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546511416075)): 8-12 year follow-up of RCT. Significant long-lasting effect of adductor training program (p=0.047 for whole group; p=0.012 for soccer players). 79% return to prior sport level.
- Frizziero et al. 2014 (PMID 24736013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bmb/ldu006)): EE promising for adductor-related groin pain, ACL rehab. Recommends standardized protocols.
- PMID 26750142 (Copenhagen adductor): Copenhagen protocol shown effective for groin injury prevention

**Copenhagen Adductor Exercise:**
- Short lever (knee on bench): entry level, phase 2-3
- Long lever (foot on bench): advanced, phase 3-4
- Shown to be effective as progressive groin strengthening
- Evidence: RCTs showing groin injury reduction in soccer

---

### 9. LUMBAR STRAIN REHABILITATION

**Core Evidence:**
- PMID 38899776, 36434687: Bird Dog and Dead Bug - McGill spine stabilization exercises. Strong evidence for core stability in lumbar rehabilitation.
- PMID 42289667: Core strengthening for lumbopelvic stability. Studies 6-12 weeks of core strengthening show significant pain reduction.
- Sutton et al. 2016 (PMID 26976373, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmpt.2016.01.004)): Multimodal care systematic review. Mobilization + stretching recommended for plantar heel pain; inconclusive for patellofemoral pain.

**Protocol:**
- Phase 1: Pelvic tilts, pain education
- Phase 2: Bird Dog, Dead Bug, Side Bridge (McGill Big Three)
- Phase 3: Romanian Deadlift, loaded spine stability
- Phase 4: Sport-specific movement patterns

### 10. EXERCISE DOSAGE SCIENCE

**General Principles (Evidence Synthesis):**

| Parameter | General Recommendation | Evidence Level |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------|
| Strength (hypertrophy) | 3-4 sets x 8-12 reps, 70-85% 1RM | A |
| Strength (endurance) | 2-3 sets x 15-20 reps, 50-65% 1RM | A |
| Isometric hold | 4-5 sets x 45 seconds | A (tendinopathy) |
| Eccentric loading | 3 sets x 15 reps, twice daily (Alfredson) | A |
| HSR tendon protocol | 3-4 sets x 6-15 reps, 70-90% 1RM | A |
| Frequency (strength) | 2-3 sessions/week with 48h recovery | A |
| Plyometrics | Begin bilateral, 2-3x/week, progress to unilateral | B |

**Pain Monitoring During Exercise:**
- Acceptable during exercise: ≤4/10 (or ≤5/10 for Alfredson protocol)
- Next-day: Must return to baseline within 24 hours
- If pain persists >24h: reduce volume/intensity by 20-30%
- Pain-free criterion for sprinting: mandatory (85.5% Delphi agreement, Paton 2023)

### 11. RETURN TO SPORT CRITERIA

**Evidence-Based Battery (Multiple Injuries):**

**Strength Tests:**
- Isokinetic dynamometry: ≥90% LSI at 60°/s and 180°/s
- Single-leg leg press LSI ≥90%
- Single-leg calf raise ≥30 reps symmetric

**Hop Tests (validated for ACL, widely used for LE injuries):**
- Single-leg hop for distance: ≥90% LSI
- Triple hop for distance: ≥90% LSI
- 6-meter timed hop: ≤10% asymmetry
- Crossover hop: ≥90% LSI
- Reference: van Melick 2016 (Consensus, 772 citations)

**Balance/Proprioception:**
- Y-Balance Test anterior reach ≥90% LSI
- Star Excursion Balance Test symmetric reach distances
- Single-leg stance: eyes closed, 30 seconds on foam

**Psychological:**
- ACL-RSI ≥65 (ACL injuries)
- Fear-Avoidance Beliefs Questionnaire if indicated
- Self-confidence ≥8/10 for sport-specific activities
- Reference: Tranaeus et al. 2024 (Consensus, 56 citations) - psychology of sport injury consensus

**Timing:**
- ACL: minimum 9-12 months (van Melick 2016)
- Hamstring: pain-free sprint criteria (mandatory) + strength symmetry
- Ankle: functional clearance via hop tests + agility testing
- Achilles: VISA-A ≥80 + pain-free running

### 12. PROGRESSIVE LOADING PRINCIPLES

**Key Papers:**
- Impellizzeri et al. 2020 (Consensus - 136 citations): Training load monitoring limitations. No evidence supports quantitative TL data to prevent injury. Clinical judgment + individual response essential.
- Silbernagel et al. 2020 (Consensus - 183 citations): Progressive tendon loading central to Achilles tendinopathy management.
- Trybulski et al. 2026 (PMID 41710438, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.52082/jssm.2026.34)): Scoping review, 31 studies. Eccentric training consistently improves pain/function in tendinopathy. Protocols varied substantially - standardization needed.

**Graded Loading Framework:**
1. Isometric → Isotonic → Eccentric → Heavy Slow Resistance → Plyometric → Sport-specific
2. Each transition requires:
   - Pain criterion (during and 24h post)
   - Strength criterion (LSI threshold)
   - Functional criterion (movement quality)

## Limitations and Caveats

1. **Heterogeneity**: Exercise protocols vary significantly across studies (Trybulski 2026). Dosage recommendations are synthesized best estimates.
2. **Pain during exercise**: Different injuries tolerate different pain levels. Tendinopathies allow more exercise pain than acute muscle strains.
3. **Individualization**: Paton 2023 Delphi (89.8%): rehabilitation should be individualized based on athlete, sport demands, muscle involved, and injury severity.
4. **Psychological factors**: Fear of re-injury can be as limiting as physical factors. Psychological readiness assessment is part of evidence-based RTS criteria (Tranaeus 2024).
5. **Sport specificity**: Exercises in this library are general; additional sport-specific exercises are required based on the individual athlete's sport demands.
