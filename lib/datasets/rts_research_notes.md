## Key Findings by Injury Type

### Hamstring Strain

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.1016/j.jbmt.2025.06.030](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2025.06.030) (PMID 40954668): Systematic review and meta-analysis (2025). L-protocol eccentric exercises significantly reduced reinjury rates (MD −1.95, 95% CI [−1.81, −0.29]) and accelerated RTP (MD −4.39 days, 95% CI [−5.67, −3.11]) vs C-protocol. Recurrence rates 12–31% in sprint sports.

- [doi:10.1177/0363546510382857](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546510382857) (PMID 21051422): 165 elite track athletes, 24-month cohort. Average RTP: Grade I = 7.4 days, Grade II = 12.9 days, Grade III = 29.5 days, Grade IV = 55.0 days. Grade II had highest recurrence (24.1%). Grade I recurrence 9.3%.

- PMID 29671490: Common Running Injuries review — acute hamstring strains require relative rest followed by stretching and graded return.

**Key clinical message:** Eccentric lengthening protocols are first-line evidence. Grade II is the highest-risk recurrence category despite shorter recovery than Grade III.

### ACL Reconstruction

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.1016/j.asmart.2026.05.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asmart.2026.05.006) (PMID 42205141): APKASS 2024 International Consensus. Key: avoid purely time-based decisions. Lachman, Pivot Shift, functional testing, and psychological readiness all required. Shared decision-making emphasized.

- [doi:10.7759/cureus.106148](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.106148) (PMID 42077697): Pediatric RTP systematic review. ACL: 9–12 months average adult; 14–16 months skeletally immature. Concussion highly variable. Protocol variation noted across provider types.

- [doi:10.1007/s00167-016-4246-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00167-016-4246-3) (PMID 27423208): Test battery for ACLR RTS. LSI >90% on isokinetic and hop tests, LESS <5, ACL-RSI >56. Only 2/28 patients passed all criteria at 6.5 months post-ACLR. Majority need extended rehabilitation.

- [doi:10.1177/03635465221125467](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465221125467) (PMID 36190172): 862 soccer players, 5-year follow-up. Graft rupture 10%, contralateral ACL 8%. Males ≤18: 44% further ACL injury. Younger age = 3–7× higher re-injury risk.

- [doi:10.1016/j.jcot.2025.103176](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcot.2025.103176) (PMID 40894294): Umbrella review 55 studies. Criteria-based > time-based protocols. LSI ≥90–95% on hop tests. ACL-RSI ≥70: OR 2.1–2.5 for successful RTS (p<0.001). BFR training: SMD 0.47–0.79 for strength.

- [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0266029](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0266029) (PMID 35325002): Network analysis of 441 ACL-RSI responses. Fear of reinjury (Q7) and relaxation about sport (Q12) are the most central nodes. Knee confidence (Q8) most important for athletes who successfully returned.

- [doi:10.4103/npmj.npmj_504_25](https://doi.org/10.4103/npmj.npmj_504_25) (PMID 42085052): RCT, functional vs traditional rehab. 48-week RTS rate: FRM 89.3% vs TRM 62.1%. Quadriceps LSI FRM: 89.15% vs TRM: 69.45%.

### Ankle Sprain

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.1080/07853890.2025.2586334](https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2025.2586334) (PMID 41385332): Systematic review (20 papers). RTS is multifaceted: injury extent, causation, treatment protocols, and recovery criteria all influence timing. Standard paradigm lacking; functional criteria identified.

- PMID 9083943, 8406246: Early functional treatment superior to cast immobilization for ankle sprain. Proprioception training key to preventing chronic instability.

### Rotator Cuff Repair

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.1016/j.xrrt.2021.04.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xrrt.2021.04.009) (PMID 37588949): Narrative review. Variable timelines based on tear size. Prognostic factors: tear size, healing rate, ROM timeline, patient compliance. Overhead throwers require ~12 months. Non-compliance → 20–40% re-tear rates.

### Patellar Tendinopathy

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.1002/ksa.70284](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.70284) (PMID 41553011): Meta-analysis (13 studies, 2074 records). Surgical VISA-P improvement WMD 40.3. Fat pad debridement adds 7.0 weeks delay to RTS (p=0.007). Arthroscopic > open surgery in RTS time. Conservative management first-line.

### Achilles Tendon Rupture

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.12968/hmed.2022.0239](https://doi.org/10.12968/hmed.2022.0239) (PMID 37235667): Review of 24 studies (947 patients). 65–100% RTS between 3–13.4 months post-surgical repair. Re-rupture rate 0–5.74%. Surgical repair preferred for high-demand athletes.

### Stress Fracture

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.2165/00007256-199724060-00006](https://doi.org/10.2165/00007256-199724060-00006) (PMID 9421865): Classic Brukner & Bennell review. Most stress fractures heal in 6–8 weeks with activity modification. High-risk locations (femoral neck, anterior tibial cortex, navicular, 2nd/5th metatarsal) require extended management and possible surgery. Female athletes with menstrual disturbances at higher risk.

### Concussion

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.1016/j.jshs.2016.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2016.11.002) (PMID 30356457): Stepwise progression protocol used by 83% of athletic trainers. Education level and concussion volume treated predicted adherence to full stepwise protocol.

- [doi:10.1097/HTR.0000000000000701](https://doi.org/10.1097/HTR.0000000000000701) (PMID 34145161): Scoping review, 896 adults post-SRC. Sleep disturbances in 9/10 studies. Heterogeneous methodology; polysomnography showed no differences. Sleep monitoring recommended during concussion recovery.

### Psychological Readiness

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- [doi:10.26603/001c.18794](https://doi.org/10.26603/001c.18794) (PMID 33604148): 92 matched ACLR patients. In-sport injuries: ACL-RSI 55.3 vs 60.8 out-of-sport (p<0.001). Lower emotional scores and higher injury-risk perception in in-sport injury group.

- [doi:10.1177/03635465231192983](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465231192983) (PMID 37681565): 143 participants at 12.5 months post-ACLR. Quadriceps LSI, activity level, knee pain, surgery timing, and fear of reinjury accounted for ~70% of sports function variance. Only hop distance weakly associated with psychological readiness.

- [doi:10.1186/s40634-022-00551-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40634-022-00551-2) (PMID 36454346): 38% of ACLR patients showed no psychological improvement at 6 months. Subjective knee function and psychological status improvements are independent pathways.

- [doi:10.1186/s13102-026-01684-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01684-y) (PMID 41943059): TSK-11 reanalysis. Optimal ACL-RSI cutoff for re-injury fear: 33.5 (sensitivity 70.6%, specificity 87.8%). Standard 38-point cutoff has poor sensitivity in pain-free athletes.

### Recovery Modifiers

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

**Nutrition:**
- [doi:10.1007/s00134-011-2288-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00134-011-2288-8) (PMID 23315753): Protein ≥1.6g/kg/day for optimal muscle protein synthesis during recovery.
- (PMID 32516908, 26553492, 21923203, 21346336): Protein, Vitamin D, and caloric adequacy all independently support recovery.

**Sleep:**
- [doi:10.1097/HTR.0000000000000701](https://doi.org/10.1097/HTR.0000000000000701) (PMID 34145161): Sleep disturbances documented in 9/10 SRC studies; monitoring recommended.
- (PMID 42188564): Sleep, nutrition, and recovery are interconnected; individualized emphasis recommended.

**Previous Injury:**
- (PMID 21051422): Grade I hamstring recurrence 9.3% vs Grade II 24.1%.
- (PMID 42221209, 27826402, 25700101): Previous injury is leading modifiable risk factor for recurrence across injury types.

**Elite vs Recreational:**
- [doi:10.1080/15438627.2025.2567353](https://doi.org/10.1080/15438627.2025.2567353) (PMID 41021821): Elite clinicians identified biopsychosocial, criteria-based, shared decision-making as gold standard. External pressures (clubs/coaches) are documented risks to premature RTS in elite sport.

## Decision Framework Summary

Based on the 2024 APKASS Consensus (PMID 42205141) and qualitative elite clinician research (PMID 41021821):

**The Three Pillars of RTS Clearance:**

1. **Physical Readiness**
   - Pain: NRS ≤1/10 (most injuries), 0/10 (ACL, high-risk fractures)
   - Strength: LSI ≥90% on isokinetic and hop testing
   - Function: Sport-specific tests passed
   - Load: Athlete can tolerate full training load without flare-up >24h

2. **Psychological Readiness**
   - ACL-RSI ≥65 (minimum), ≥70 (optimal)
   - TSK-11 <37 (≤33.5 for ACL re-injury fear)
   - Athlete self-reports confidence ≥8/10 in their sport
   - No unresolved anxiety, depression, or avoidance behaviors

3. **Contextual Readiness**
   - Shared decision by athlete + clinician + coach
   - Sport-specific risk acknowledged and accepted
   - Load management plan for first 4–6 weeks post-RTS
   - Injury prevention program in place
   - Return is sport-specific (not just to "exercise")
